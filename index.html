Trade-group & QRI watch

Confirmed filings in the docket as of Sep 28, 2026 (unchanged from Sep 27). IDs are regulations.gov comment numbers (FDA-2026-N-4699-xxxx).

PhRMA trade group

Comment -0191 · received Aug 24 (deadline day) · posted Aug 26 · PDF letter

"Strongly supports" accelerating FIH trials, but says it is "currently unclear how the proposed use of QRIs will accelerate" them — QRI involvement "must be appropriately scoped." Key asks:

  • Clear, published QRI qualification/certification standards (incl. safety-pharmacology, GLP, data-security criteria; modality specialization)
  • Clarify QRI interaction with pre-IND meetings; QRI advice non-binding; CMC review and all regulatory decisions stay with FDA
  • Rolling submission + review = "the most impactful element"; wants module-level timelines and a two-track design isolating rolling review from QRI effects
  • Voluntary participation, broad product eligibility; robust COI screening; no "de facto premium pathway"; broader IND/IRB modernization beyond the pilot

BIO trade group

Comment -0150 · dated Aug 20 · posted Aug 26 · PDF letter (Kolbe / Naik)

Supports the goal but is skeptical of the QRI layer: "not yet clear that adding a third-party layer … will meaningfully improve timelines beyond … more direct, FDA-led approaches." Key asks:

  • Pursue rolling review decoupled from QRI review; convene a public workshop on the workflow
  • Narrow QRI scope to submission review — decouple from IRB/site functions; no self-owned IRB/site requirement; allow consortia
  • Equity: fee-for-service QRIs risk a "de facto premium pathway"; co-application requirement may exclude small / first-time sponsors — wants matching support
  • Expedited INDs should differ from standard INDs in process only, not evidentiary standards; per-component success metrics

QRI pairing market forms new this check

Execution phase — sponsor–QRI matchmaking ahead of the Oct 30 application deadline
  • Duke Health (ORAQ) is soliciting sponsors for a joint application: interest form due Oct 2, 5 p.m. ET; Duke's selection decisions targeted ~Oct 9; chosen sponsors file a paired sponsor–QRI package to FDA by Oct 30
  • FDA launch-day remarks (Endpoints): QRI eligibility is deliberately non-prescriptive — institutions may be "private or public, academic or not"; FDA wants a diverse institutional mix to test which profiles perform
  • Expect other AMCs and CROs to stand up similar matchmaking processes in October — the first real-world test of PhRMA/BIO's access and "premium pathway" concerns

What to watch next

  • Application window (through Oct 30): which sponsors pair with which institutions — first real test of the PhRMA/BIO "premium pathway" and matching-access concerns. Duke's Oct 2 interest form is the first visible AMC matchmaking cutoff.
  • NIH results-sharing policy (comments Oct 26): the patient-engagement prong of TrialBlazer — plain-language summary results to participants, generally within a year of primary completion.
  • QRI criteria in practice: FDA's final eligibility/prioritization and COI requirements are on the pilot webpage; compare against PhRMA's certification-standard asks and BIO's consortium flexibility asks.
  • Dec 18 cohort: 8–10 pairs; watch sponsor size mix (small biotech vs. large pharma) and institution type (AMC vs. CRO vs. network).
  • NAMs rule docket (comments Dec 7): the Nonclinical Testing Terminology direct final rule is withdrawable on significant adverse comment; pairs with the QSP/MABEL draft guidance on FIH dose selection.
  • Longer arc: FDA says the pilot may inform a formal QRI accreditation model; FY2027 budget request already asks Congress to codify an expedited IND pathway. PDUFA reauthorization (US Phase 1 fee incentive) is the parallel lever.
