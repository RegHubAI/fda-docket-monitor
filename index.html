About the program: Operation TrialBlazer & the Expedited IND Pilot

Operation TrialBlazer, announced by HHS on June 22, 2026, is the administration's push to keep early-stage drug development anchored in the United States. FDA frames the effort against a competitive backdrop: in 2021 China overtook the U.S. in global share of Phase 1 clinical trials, and from 2020–2025 the eleven largest pharmaceutical companies spent over $150 billion accessing early drug assets developed in China. The initiative pairs this Expedited IND pilot RFI with practical IND infrastructure — Phase 1 CMC clarifications, an IND Navigator page, and a Phase 1 Contact Center — all aimed at compressing the path from drug identification to first-in-human (FIH) study.

The pilot itself would stand up a network of Qualified Research Institutions (QRIs) — academic medical centers, healthcare networks, CROs, regulatory advisors, and similar organizations — that partner with sponsors to develop and pre-review the pharmacology/toxicology, clinical, and CMC components of Phase 1 INDs. Three mechanisms are being tested: raising submission quality to reduce Phase 1 clinical holds; a rolling submission platform that lets FDA review completed IND components as they arrive (with the 30-day review clock starting only when the final component lands, and a "safe to proceed" potentially issued sooner); and running trial-startup activities such as IRB review and site contracting in parallel with IND development. QRIs advise only — FDA retains full regulatory authority, including clinical holds and inspections, and sponsors keep ownership of their INDs. If the pilot succeeds, FDA expects to refine its IND review process more broadly; participation is voluntary, and after the pilot sponsors would pay QRIs directly for their services.

Source: RFI notice, 91 FR 37996 (Jun 24, 2026), § I Background and § II.A Proposed Pilot Structure.
